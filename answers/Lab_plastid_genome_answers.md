**3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.**

The IRb is located on the lower-left side and IRa is on the lower-right side. These four regions make up the major structural parts of the circular plastid genome.

One example is ndhF, which is located in the SSC region.

One example is rrn16. Yes, it is shown more than once because the inverted repeat regions are duplicated, so genes located within them can have two copies. 

**7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.** 
One photosynthesis-related gene is psbA. It codes for the photosystem II D1 protein, which is involved in photosynthesis and the light-dependent reactions. 

**8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?** 
The arrows show the direction in which the genes are transcribed. Genes with arrows pointing in different directions are oriented differently on the plastid genome. 

**9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions? Describe what you observe without over-interpreting it.** 
The GC content does not appear completely uniform across the entire genome. The graph shows some variation in GC content among different regions, with some areas having slightly higher or lower values. 

**10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?** 
A graphical map makes it easier to see the organization of the plastid genome. It allows us to quickly identify the LSC, SSC, IR regions, genes, gene orientation, and GC content instead of having to examine a long DNA sequence or list of annotations.
